date-arithmetic (warning): Adding numbers to dates

A date plus 1 is the next day on PostgreSQL, the number 20240132 on MySQL and
MariaDB, and 2025 on SQLite.

MySQL and MariaDB turn the date into the number 20240131 and add 1. SQLite
turns the text into the number 2024. PostgreSQL adds a day. Subtracting two
dates goes the same way: PostgreSQL counts days and MySQL and MariaDB subtract
the numbers. INTERVAL arithmetic works on PostgreSQL, MySQL and MariaDB, with
different result types, and SQLite has no INTERVAL.

Example:
    SELECT CAST('2024-01-31' AS DATE) + 1

    SQLite 3.45.1        2025
    PostgreSQL 16.15     2024-02-01
    MySQL 8.0.46         20240132
    MariaDB 10.11.14     20240132

No date arithmetic reads the same on all four engines. Use date(d, '+1 day')
on SQLite and d + INTERVAL '1' DAY on the other three, or do the arithmetic in
the application.

              SQLite PostgreSQL         MySQL      MariaDB
date + number 2025   2024-02-01         20240132   20240132
INTERVAL      no     2024-02-01 00:0... 2024-02-01 2024-02-01
